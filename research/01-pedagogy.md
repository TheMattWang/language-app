# Pedagogy & Product Thesis — SLA Research for a Forcing-Function Spanish App

Research pass for Matt Wang's Spanish-practice app (on-device SLM + RL adaptation, MacBook Air M3, Spain trip forthcoming). Core thesis under test: **language skill decays unless purposefully forced into production.**

Confidence tags used throughout: **[well-established]** — converging evidence across multiple study designs / meta-analyses; **[contested]** — real researchers disagree, or effect sizes are small/mixed; **[speculative]** — plausible extrapolation, thin direct evidence.

---

## 1. Attrition: what actually happens to a forgotten L2

**The curve is Ebbinghaus-shaped but not simple exponential decay.** Attrition is fastest immediately after exposure stops, decelerates in a middle plateau, then in some longitudinal studies re-accelerates in later stages ("swift-slow-swift" pattern) — a refinement of the classic Ebbinghaus forgetting curve applied to language-specific longitudinal data. [contested — the later-stage re-acceleration is reported in some multi-year studies, not a settled universal] Source: [Language attrition guest editorial](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09571736.2024.2307641), [Bridging SLA and memory science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6866255/).

**Threshold / plateau effects are real but messy.** The "Regression Hypothesis" (last learned, first forgotten — Jakobson) and a related **critical-threshold intuition** show up across studies: learners who reach a higher stabilized proficiency before the exposure gap show markedly less attrition than those who stop at a lower level, and some domains (grammar competence, deeply automatized routines) resist decay far more than others (spontaneous lexical retrieval). But results are genuinely mixed: some studies find **no attrition or even apparent gains** after 1.5–2 years of reduced exposure, particularly in listening/reading comprehension for learners who had immersion experience — plausibly because underlying competence was never lost, only access speed, and some skills continue consolidating offline. [contested] Source: [Interactions between proficiency and length of exposure to lexical attrition](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12483873/), [Bridging SLA and memory science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6866255/).

**Productive vs. receptive decay rates diverge — this is the load-bearing finding for Matt's thesis.** Across the attrition literature, productive/active vocabulary and fluent spontaneous production degrade markedly faster than receptive comprehension and passive recognition. This is the single strongest piece of direct support for "use it or lose it, must force production": if receptive skill (a Netflix-and-input-only strategy) survived attrition as well as productive skill does, there'd be no case for forcing output. It doesn't survive as well. **[well-established]** — this asymmetry (production >> comprehension in decay rate) recurs across the attrition and bilingualism literature and is consistent with general memory-science findings that retrieval/production is a stronger encoding-and-maintenance event than passive exposure (see §3, testing effect). Source: [Bridging SLA and memory science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6866255/), [Second Language Attrition overview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304041358_Second_Language_Attrition).

**Where Matt's intuition oversimplifies:**
- **"Use it or lose it" is directionally right but the mechanism isn't pure disuse — it's retrieval strength decay, not storage-strength deletion.** Underlying representations (esp. grammar, phonology once automatized) often survive far longer than accessibility does; relearning after attrition is reliably faster than original learning (savings effect), meaning the app's real job during a lapse-recovery period is *fast reactivation drilling*, not re-teaching from scratch. **[well-established: savings-in-relearning is one of the most robust findings in the memory literature generally]**
- **A single trip to Spain won't "fix" attrition long-term** — gains from immersion often partially regress once the learner returns to an L1-dominant environment, unless a maintenance-frequency floor of production is kept up. This argues for the app's post-trip role (maintenance mode) being as important as its pre-trip role (acquisition/forcing mode).
- **Not all skills decay at the same rate**, so a single "your Spanish is decaying" meter is wrong: pronunciation/phonology and highly automatized formulaic chunks (greetings, ordering, survival phrases) are attrition-resistant; free lexical retrieval and complex morphosyntax (subjunctive, clitic placement) are attrition-fragile. A forgetting model should be **per-skill-domain**, not global.

## 2. Forced output: Swain, Krashen, and where 2020s consensus actually lands

**Krashen's Input Hypothesis (comprehensible input, i+1)** claims acquisition happens through understanding input slightly above current level; production is a byproduct, not a cause, of competence. **Swain's Output Hypothesis** (from watching Canadian French-immersion students who had years of rich input but plateaued well below native production ability) argues output does something input cannot: it forces the learner to move from semantic ("gist") processing to full **syntactic processing**, exposes gaps ("I don't actually know how to say this"), lets the learner test hypotheses against feedback, and builds metalinguistic/automatized fluency through practice itself. Source: [Comprehensible output — overview](https://grokipedia.com/page/Comprehensible_output), [Swain output hypothesis explainer](https://www.scribd.com/document/400728998/Swain-vs-Krashen-Second-Language-Acquisition).

**Current consensus (2020s): input and output are complementary, not competing, and the "debate" is largely resolved in textbooks — but a real empirical fight persists underneath.** [well-established that the field has moved past treating them as opposed] Mainstream SLA now treats comprehensible input as necessary-but-insufficient: the immersion-plateau evidence that motivated Swain (comprehension-only learners plateau below native-like production, particularly on morphosyntax) has never been overturned and is one of the more replicated findings in the field. Recent critiques (2020s neuro-ecological papers) go further and argue input-only accounts underweight embodied/interactive and production-driven mechanisms entirely. [contested — this is a smaller, more polemical strand, not mainstream consensus] Source: [Neuro-ecological critique of Krashen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12577063/).

**What this means concretely for the app:** input-only (reading/listening to Spanish) is necessary for vocabulary breadth and pattern exposure but **will plateau** a learner on production, exactly matching Matt's intuition — this is Swain's original finding, not a hunch. Pure output drilling without input is also wrong (no vocabulary/grammar to output). The evidence supports a **cycle**: input → noticing a gap → pushed output → corrective feedback → re-input. This directly justifies an architecture where the model doesn't just chat, but actively pushes the learner past their comfort zone ("pushed output" — Swain's specific term for output that is slightly beyond current ability, elicited by task demands or explicit prompting for more precision) and then feeds back corrected/enriched input.

**Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT)** operationalizes pushed output via tasks with real (or simulated) communicative outcomes rather than form-focused drills. Meta-analytic evidence for TBLT's speaking-proficiency benefit is **positive but the field's own 2023 meta-analysis meta-analysis is cautious about effect-size inflation** — "designed programs based on TBLT procedures enhanced productive skills better than traditional teaching," but a systematic 2023 review (Boers & Faez) explicitly asks "are we there yet?" and flags heterogeneity in what counts as a TBLT program and weak control conditions in many studies. **[contested — direction of effect is fairly solid, magnitude is not well pinned down]** Source: [TBLT meta-analysis, Boers & Faez 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13621688231167573), [TBLT effectiveness systematic review](https://www.ijlter.org/index.php/ijlter/article/view/15904).

## 3. Retrieval practice, spacing, and why Anki-style SRS is a poor fit for speaking

**The testing/retrieval-practice effect and spacing effect are among the most robust findings in all of cognitive psychology** [well-established], packaged by Bjork as "desirable difficulties" — conditions that feel harder in the moment (spacing, interleaving, contextual variation, generation before feedback) but produce durable, transferable learning, versus massed/blocked practice that feels fluent during study but doesn't stick. Source: [Bjork desirable difficulties](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/robert-bjork-teachers-guide-desirable), [Desirable Difficulties and studying in the region of proximal learning](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/metcalfe/PDFs/Metcalfe-BjorkVolSubmitFeb14Final.pdf). A 2024/2025 study specifically testing whether *making L2 vocabulary learning harder* enhances retention and transfer found supportive evidence for this in vocabulary specifically. Source: [Making L2 vocabulary learning difficult](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12108878/).

**FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler)** models memory with three latent variables — Retrievability, Stability, Difficulty — and schedules reviews to hit a target retrievability, outperforming SM-2 (Anki's legacy default) on real-world retention data. [well-established as an algorithmic improvement over SM-2] Source: [FSRS explained](https://anki-decks.com/blog/post/anki-fsrs-explained/), [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md).

**The critical failure mode for this app: SRS algorithms (SM-2, FSRS alike) were built for and validated on discrete-fact recall (a card has a right answer), not productive, generative, context-dependent speech.** Concretely:
- **Recognition-masquerading-as-recall.** The most commonly cited Anki failure mode is grading recognition ("that looks familiar") as if it were recall. This gets *worse*, not better, when the "item" is a grammatical construction or a spoken utterance rather than a word — a learner can feel fluent recognizing "no lo hubiera hecho" and still be unable to generate a novel subjunctive-past-perfect sentence under time pressure. **A speaking-oriented system must grade generation, not recognition-of-familiarity — the interval-update signal has to come from actually producing under mild time pressure, not from self-rated "did I know this."** [well-established as a documented flashcard failure mode; the SRS-for-speech extension is speculative but the underlying logic transfers directly]
- **Item atomicity breaks down.** SM-2/FSRS assume "the item" is a fixed, well-defined unit reviewed the same way each time. Productive speech skill isn't item-shaped — the same grammatical construction (e.g., ser vs. estar, subjunctive triggers) needs to be practiced across many different lexical/contextual instantiations to actually transfer, not the same flashcard reviewed at increasing intervals. Pure SRS on fixed sentences risks **item-specific memorization** (the learner "knows" that one sentence) without construction-level generalization. This argues for interleaving *constructions* across varying content (see interleaving, above) rather than treating a construction like a vocabulary card.
- **No SRS captures "can produce it live, under time pressure, in a novel context"** — that's a different construct (proceduralized/automatized skill) from "can recall it given unlimited time to think," which is what most SRS reviews actually test. Fluency automatization research (see task list in §5) suggests this needs *timed, repeated, task-based* practice (e.g., 4/3/2 technique — same content retold in decreasing time limits) rather than card review at all. [contested — this is an inference from the fluency-automatization literature more than a direct SRS-for-speaking study, since that literature is thin]
- Practically: cap new-item introduction (the 10–20/day heuristic reported for FSRS) applies just as much or more to productive constructions, where overload is worse because generation is more effortful than recognition. Source: [Migaku spaced repetition 2026 explainer](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/spaced-repetition-in-2026-how-it-actually-works), [Domenic Denicola on FSRS](https://domenic.me/fsrs/).

## 4. Affective filter, Willingness to Communicate, and the AI-conversation-partner question

**MacIntyre et al.'s (1998) Pyramid Model of WTC** is the field's dominant framework: six layers running from stable trait-level factors (personality, intergroup attitudes, L2 self-confidence) at the base up through situational/state factors (desire to speak to this specific person right now, state anxiety, state self-confidence) to the immediate behavioral decision to communicate. The key implication: **WTC is not a fixed trait — it fluctuates turn-by-turn based on situational self-confidence and anxiety**, which is exactly the variable a conversation-partner design can manipulate. [well-established as the field's standard model] Source: [Wikipedia: Willingness to communicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willingness_to_communicate), [Pyramid model application study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13621688211004645).

**Do AI partners lower anxiety vs. humans? Yes, fairly consistently, across a growing 2024–2026 literature.** Multiple recent studies (mixed-methods and quantitative) on AI chatbot conversation partners for L2/EFL learners report: increased WTC, reduced foreign-language speaking anxiety (FLSA), increased self-perceived communicative competence, and the mechanism is explicitly the **non-judgmental, infinitely patient, low-social-stakes** nature of the interlocutor. One study found affective factors (speaking enjoyment strongest, then self-confidence, then grit) explained 83.9% of variance in WTC with AI. A 2026 study specifically on **AI-assisted Spanish speaking** found positive effects on foreign-language emotions and WTC. **[well-established direction of effect, though most of these studies are self-report / short intervention, not long-horizon RCTs]** Sources: [AI chatbots reducing speaking anxiety, Nature Humanities & Social Sciences Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05550-z), [AI conversational agents and FLSA, Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1799269/full), [AI chatbots, WTC, FLSA and self-perceived competence](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X24003154), [Drivers of WTC with generative AI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12883774/), [AI-assisted Spanish speaking and WTC, Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1877956/full).

**The cost — does low-stakes practice transfer to high-stakes real use? This is the field's biggest open question, and it matters enormously for the "forcing function" thesis.** [contested/speculative — I could not find a strong longitudinal transfer study specifically testing AI-practice → real-world-stakes performance] The theoretical worry, consistent with desirable-difficulties logic (§3) and with basic transfer-of-training research generally: if the AI partner is *too* comfortable, it risks becoming an anxiety-reducing crutch that never actually rehearses the learner's ability to perform **under the exact conditions that will occur in Spain** — real time pressure, real social cost of embarrassment, unpredictable accents/speech rate, no pause button. Low-anxiety practice is good for building the underlying competence and self-confidence *precondition* for WTC, but if the app never simulates any stakes (time pressure, consequence for failure, unpredictability), it risks producing a learner who is fluent with a patient AI and freezes with a rapid-fire Madrid waiter. **Design implication (see §8): deliberately escalate stakes/pressure over the course of training — don't hold the AI partner at a permanently low-anxiety setting.**

## 5. Concrete forcing-function mechanics: 10–15 designs, evidence, auto-eval difficulty

| # | Mechanic | What it trains | Evidence base | Auto-eval difficulty (for a local SLM) |
|---|----------|-----------------|----------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Time-pressured roleplay** (order food in 30s, negotiate a price before a countdown) | Fluency under stress, automatization, real WTC-under-pressure | Consistent with TBLT + desirable-difficulty logic; direct RCTs specifically on timed roleplay are thin [contested] | Medium — needs turn-taking + latency + basic task-completion check; model can score "did they accomplish the transactional goal" |
| 2 | **Information-gap tasks** (you have the map, partner has the destination; must ask questions to complete) | Negotiation of meaning, question formation, spontaneous production | [well-established] — info-gap tasks specifically show more meaning-negotiation than opinion-gap tasks | Medium — needs to verify the gap was actually closed (info transferred correctly), doable with structured hidden state |
| 3 | **"Must resolve to exit" scenario** (lost luggage, wrong hotel booking — task fails if not resolved in target language) | Real-stakes goal-directed communication, sustained multi-turn production | Direct evidence sparse but aligns with TBLT "outcome-based assessment" principle [contested] | Hard — requires tracking a mutable scenario state machine + judging whether the *content* (not just fluency) actually resolved it |
| 4 | **Constrained-vocabulary tasks** ("describe this without using the word X", survival-Spanish-only mode) | Circumlocution, paraphrase strategy — a core real-world survival skill | Aligns with communicative-strategy research (compensatory strategies) [contested — less core-SLA literature than the others, more strategy-training literature] | Easy-medium — vocabulary-list matching to detect banned-word violations is straightforward; judging paraphrase *quality* is harder |
| 5 | **Shadowing** (listen + repeat simultaneously, matching rhythm/intonation) | Phonology, prosody, phonological loop capacity, listening-speaking integration | [well-established for pronunciation/fluency gains, esp. East Asian L2 English literature] | Medium-hard — needs ASR + prosody/timing comparison, not just word-error-rate; feasible on-device with forced alignment |
| 6 | **Elicited imitation** (hear a sentence above current ability, immediately repeat it) | Diagnostic proxy for underlying grammatical competence (this is actually a *measurement* tool as much as a training one) | [well-established as a *validated proficiency measure* — strong construct validity evidence across many studies] | Easy-medium — scoring is literally "how close was the reproduction," well-suited to automated diff/ASR scoring, and doubles as a CEFR-estimation input (§7) |
| 7 | **Retelling / narrative retell** (watch or hear a story, retell it in your own words) | Discourse-level coherence, macro-structure, vocabulary retrieval under content constraint | [well-established, esp. for intermediate-level learners; medium-duration interventions outperform short or very long ones per 2025 meta-analysis] | Hard — content-fidelity + coherence scoring is a genuine LLM-judge task, feasible but needs careful rubric design to avoid rewarding verbosity |
| 8 | **Spontaneous monologue with topic cards** (60-90s uninterrupted talk on a random prompt) | Fluency, spontaneous lexical access, coping with topic unpredictability — closest analog to "get put on the spot" | Aligns with fluency-measurement literature (speech rate, pause frequency as proficiency proxies, §7) [well-established as a *measurement* paradigm, less so validated as a *training* method per se] | Medium — pause/rate/TTR metrics are cheap to compute locally; content quality scoring is the harder part |
| 9 | **4/3/2 technique** (retell the same content in 4 min, then 3, then 2 — forces automatization via repetition + shrinking time) | Fluency automatization specifically (this is the closest thing to solving the "SRS doesn't work for speaking" gap in §3) | [contested but well-known in fluency-focused TBLT literature — direct effect studies exist but are not huge in number] | Medium — can literally track speech-rate increase and disfluency decrease across the three attempts as the reward signal, which is unusually clean |
| 10 | **Opinion-gap / debate tasks** (defend a position, respond to pushback) | Argumentation, real-time counter-response, complex syntax under pressure | [well-established as a TBLT task type; shown to elicit different — more negotiation-light but complexity-heavy — output than info-gap tasks] | Hard — evaluating argument quality + linguistic form simultaneously is a genuine LLM-judge challenge |
| 11 | **Register-switching drills** (same request, formal vs. informal — tú vs. usted, ordering at a Michelin restaurant vs. a bar) | Pragmatic competence, sociolinguistic appropriateness — critical for a Spain trip specifically | Thin direct SLA-effectiveness literature; grounded mainly in pragmatics/sociolinguistics theory [speculative as a *trained* skill, though clearly relevant to real use] | Hard to auto-evaluate well — register appropriateness is subtle, easy to get false positives/negatives from a small on-device model |
| 12 | **Error-correction / noticing tasks** (learner hears their own recorded error played back, must self-correct) | Noticing-the-gap (directly operationalizes Swain's mechanism), metalinguistic awareness | Directly derived from Output Hypothesis's "noticing" function [well-established as a theoretical mechanism; direct RCT effect sizes vary] | Medium — ASR + diff against a corrected version is tractable |
| 13 | **Comprehension-checked interaction** (partner deliberately misunderstands unless the learner is precise enough — a real interlocutor consequence) | Communicative precision, output pushed by actual communication breakdown, not artificial task rules | Directly modeled on how negotiation-of-meaning naturally arises in TBLT info-gap research [well-established mechanism, less validated as a discrete standalone drill] | Medium — the model already "is" the interlocutor, so triggering feigned misunderstanding on ambiguous input is a design choice, not an eval problem |
| 14 | **Survival-scenario stakes escalation** (a running storyline where consequences compound — miss the train because you failed to ask in time) | Realistic consequence-linked motivation, addresses the transfer-to-high-stakes gap flagged in §4 | Speculative — game-based/narrative-consequence language learning has some support in gamification literature but not much rigorous SLA-specific causal evidence [speculative] | Hard — requires persistent state + narrative consistency, a genuine engineering lift beyond pedagogy |
| 15 | **Timed "phone call" simulation (voice-only, no visual/text crutch, real turn-taking latency)** | Removes the biggest artificial crutch of app-based practice (visible text, unlimited think-time); closest proxy to the actual Spain scenario | Not a named SLA technique per se, but directly implied by ecological-validity concerns in the WTC/anxiety-transfer literature (§4) [speculative but low-risk, high-face-validity] | Medium — needs real-time ASR/TTS pipeline (feasible on M3 with a small enough model) and turn-taking latency enforcement |

## 6. Spain-specific curriculum priorities

For a traveler going to Spain (not a heritage learner, not going long-term to Latin America), a general "neutral Latin American Spanish" course (the Duolingo/most-app default) actively mis-trains several things a traveler will hit constantly:

- **Distinción (c/z → [θ], "th" sound) vs. seseo.** Most apps and most online content default to Latin American seseo. In Spain, distinción is the norm outside parts of Andalusia/Canary Islands. **Priority: high** — mishearing/misproducing this is one of the fastest "tourist tells" and affects listening comprehension of authentic Spain audio (waiters, transit announcements). Source: [FluentU Castilian vs LatAm](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/spanish/differences-between-castilian-and-latin-american-spanish/), [Spanish.academy 10 differences](https://www.spanish.academy/blog/differences-between-latin-american-and-castilian-spanish/).
- **Vosotros conjugation.** Nearly universal in Spain for informal plural "you" (vs. ustedes-only in Latin America). A traveler who only knows ustedes will *understand* Spaniards fine but will sound foreign and, worse, will fail to parse vosotros verb endings (-áis, -éis, -asteis) in fast native speech — a real comprehension gap, not just a production nicety. **Priority: high for listening comprehension, medium for production** (production mistakes here are low-stakes; comprehension gaps are not).
- **Leísmo.** Widely accepted/normal in central and northern Spain (using "le" for direct objects, especially for people) — a traveler taught strict lo/la rules from a Latin-American-normed course may find natural Spain speech to sound "wrong" per their training, causing confusion rather than any real communication failure. **Priority: low-medium** — mainly a "don't be confused/thrown off, don't correct a Spaniard" awareness item, not something to actively drill.
- **Vocabulary divergences that matter for a traveler**: coche (not carro), ordenador (not computadora), zumo (not jugo), móvil (not celular), conducir (not manejar), tarta (not pastel/torta in most contexts), aparcar (not estacionar), and the big transactional one — vale as the all-purpose "okay." **Priority: high** — these are exactly the words a traveler needs in the first hour (transport, phone, ordering) and getting them "wrong" (LatAm variant) doesn't break communication but immediately reads as non-local and can cause momentary confusion in fast transactional exchanges.
- **Pragmatics specific to Spain**: tuteo is far more pervasive and used faster with strangers in Spain than in most of Latin America (using "tú" quickly with service staff, peers) — a learner over-trained on formal "usted" registers from a generic course will sound stiff/overly formal. Bar/tapas ordering culture has its own script (ordering at the barra, paying norms, "¿Qué le pongo?", "una caña", "de nada" vs. no response at all) that's absent from generic transactional-Spanish units. **Priority: high** — this is exactly the kind of real forcing-function content (§5, register-switching + scenario tasks) the app should build scenario content around, since it's high-frequency and travel-specific.
- **Regional variation within Spain**: Madrid Spanish is closest to a "standard" reference accent; Andalusian speech drops final -s and -d, aspirates/elides consonants heavily (noticeably faster and more reduced — genuinely harder listening comprehension, and a traveler visiting Andalusia specifically should be warned this is a comprehension spike, not that they've regressed); Catalonia has widespread Catalan-Spanish bilingual contact affecting some vocabulary/pronunciation in Barcelona particularly, though Castilian Spanish remains fully functional there; Basque Country similarly has bilingual contact effects but Spanish is fully dominant for a traveler's purposes. **Priority: medium** — worth a short "if you're headed to Andalusia, expect this" module rather than deep curriculum coverage, since it's exposure/expectation-setting more than a trainable production skill.

**Overall recommendation**: default the app's TTS/ASR and vocabulary bank to **Peninsular/Castilian norms** (distinción, vosotros, Spain vocabulary) rather than the LatAm-default most apps ship with, since Matt is specifically going to Spain — this is a real, cheap, high-leverage curriculum decision most general-purpose apps get wrong for this exact use case.

## 7. Proficiency measurement: a spoken CEFR placement probe, and reward-signal gameability

**Why this section got promoted.** Matt doesn't know his own CEFR level. Every downstream design decision — how hard to make the first scenario, which constructions to drill, what "pushed" output even means for this specific learner — depends on a starting estimate. This has to be **the first thing the product does**, not a nice-to-have analytics layer, and it has to run in ~5 minutes, fully on-device (M3, no phone, no cloud API, no native speaker to grade it).

### 7.1 The elicitation battery

No single task type has been validated at exactly 5-minute duration against CEFR — that specific combination (short-form + CEFR + Spanish) does not appear to have a dedicated study I could find. What follows is a battery assembled from tasks that individually have strong validity evidence at longer durations, budgeted down to fit 5 minutes. **This composition itself is an extrapolation [speculative]**, even though every component task is well-evidenced in isolation.

| Step | Task | Duration | What it targets | Evidence for the task type (not the 5-min duration specifically) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **C-test** (5-6 short damaged-text passages, restore deleted letters) | ~90s | Reading/processing speed, vocabulary depth, morphosyntactic knowledge — fast proxy for general proficiency | **[well-established]** C-tests correlate .5–.8 with full placement batteries, ≤15 min in standard form and compressible further since it's the cheapest-per-item signal here; also the only *purely automatable, unambiguous-answer* task in the battery (exact-string scoring, no ASR or judgment needed) — Source: standard C-test placement literature |
| 2 | **Elicited imitation** (8-10 sentences of increasing length/complexity, 7-19 syllables, hear once, 2-3s pause, repeat) | ~90s | Underlying grammatical competence, working-memory-mediated processing of morphosyntax the learner hasn't automatized | **[well-established as a construct]** — validated proficiency measure across languages (Korean/TOPIK, English); fluency + accuracy of reproduction jointly predict proficiency. **[speculative]**: no direct evidence an 8-10 item / 90s version (vs. the standard 30-item format) preserves the same predictive validity — treat this as a plausible but unverified compression |
| 3 | **Picture description** (describe an image, unscripted) | ~60s | Spontaneous lexical access, basic descriptive syntax, fluency under mild time pressure | **[well-established]** as an oral-proficiency elicitation format used to build fluency-rating corpora; **[speculative]** at 60s specifically (typical corpora use longer prompts) |
| 4 | **Spontaneous topic monologue** (talk about a random travel-relevant prompt, e.g. "describe your ideal day in Madrid") | ~60-90s | Discourse coherence, sustained production, topic-unpredictability handling — closest proxy to "put on the spot," directly tests the forcing-function thesis itself | **[well-established as a measurement paradigm]** (speech rate, pause frequency, TTR-style metrics are validated proficiency correlates measured this way); content-quality scoring is separately the hardest part to automate (see 7.3) |

Total: ~5 minutes including brief instructions. Order matters: cheapest/most-objective task first (C-test) so a partial/aborted session still yields a usable signal.

### 7.2 What the probe outputs

Not a single number. Output:
- **A starting CEFR band** (e.g., "A2, likely low-A2 to high-A2") — a band, not a point estimate, because at 5 minutes the confidence interval is wide, especially at level boundaries where even human raters disagree (§7.4's ML-classifier numbers already degrade at B2/C1; a much shorter automated probe should be assumed noisier still).
- **A weak-area profile**, not just a level — e.g., "receptive vocabulary above the C-test's estimated level, elicited-imitation reproduction breaks down on subjunctive and clitic-pronoun-cluster sentences specifically, speech rate in the monologue task is well below the syllables/min band typical for the estimated level (processing-speed lag, not vocabulary gap)." This is what actually drives curriculum sequencing (§8) — the level number calibrates difficulty, the weak-area profile calibrates *what* to drill first.

### 7.3 Scoring: what's automatable on-device vs. what needs a human/gold reference

Given the constraint (no cloud, no native speaker in the loop), every scoring step below must run locally on the M3. That rules out sending audio to a cloud ASR/grading API — the pipeline has to be: **on-device ASR (a local Whisper-class model, e.g. whisper.cpp / MLX Whisper small-or-base, quantized) → text-level scoring**, with a few steps that can also use audio-level features directly (pause timing, speech rate) without needing ASR to be perfect.

- **Fully automatable, high confidence, on-device:**
  - C-test scoring — exact string match against a fixed answer key. No ASR, no LLM, no judgment call. Trivial to run entirely offline.
  - Speech rate / pause frequency / pause duration in the monologue and picture-description tasks — computed directly from audio (voice-activity detection + syllable/word counting from the ASR transcript), doesn't require the transcript to be perfect since it's a timing signal, not a content signal.
  - Elicited-imitation reproduction fidelity — ASR-transcribe the repetition, diff against the target sentence (edit distance / word-level match), which is a well-suited automatable scoring approach per the EI literature. Degrades gracefully even with imperfect ASR since it's a *comparison*, not open-ended judgment.

- **Automatable but needs deliberate anti-leniency design (this is the risky part):**
  - Content-quality / coherence scoring of the picture description and monologue (did they actually describe the picture accurately, is the monologue coherent) requires an LLM-as-judge step, since there's no fixed answer key. **A local small model grading its own conversation partner's elicited output is exactly the sycophancy-prone setup the LLM-judge calibration literature warns about** — general findings (not CEFR-speech-specific, that combination isn't directly studied) recommend: (a) **binary or low-precision rubric dimensions** ("did they mention at least 3 distinct picture elements: yes/no" rather than "rate coherence 1-10") — coarse scales are more reliable for LLM judges than fine-grained numeric ones; (b) **locked, evidence-anchored rubrics with few-shot calibration examples baked into the prompt** (e.g., 2-3 example transcripts at each CEFR band, sourced once from public CEFR-tagged corpora, hardcoded into the scoring prompt) rather than open-ended holistic "how good is this" judgment; (c) **never let the same model instance that generated the conversational turns also freely grade them** — use a separate, fixed scoring pass/prompt, ideally a distinct small model if two fit in memory, or at minimum a structurally different prompt role; (d) **cross-check the LLM's holistic impression against the objective features it can't easily fake** (speech rate, C-test score, EI fidelity) — if the LLM-judge score and the objective features disagree sharply, trust the objective features and flag the discrepancy rather than silently averaging them.
  - **[gap, explicitly flagged]**: I found no study directly validating LLM-judge CEFR-speaking scores against human raters. Treat any local-model CEFR score from the monologue/picture-description tasks as **provisional and secondary** to the C-test + EI fidelity signals, which have real construct-validity evidence behind them.

- **Would ideally have a human/gold reference, and doesn't, per the constraint:**
  - True CEFR calibration (does "the model said B1" actually mean DELE-B1) would normally be validated against human-rated or gold-labeled speech samples. With no native speaker in the loop, the closest available substitute is: **hardcode a small set of publicly available CEFR-tagged Spanish speech/text exemplars (e.g., from existing L2 Spanish learner corpora or CEFR-aligned reading passages converted to the C-test format) directly into the scoring prompts and thresholds as static reference points**, rather than trusting the model's free-floating notion of what B1 sounds like. This is a one-time setup cost (curate ~2-3 exemplars per level, per task type) that meaningfully de-risks the leniency problem without violating the offline/no-human-in-the-loop constraint — it's a *fixed asset* shipped with the app, not a live human rater.
  - Ongoing recalibration (is the model's B1 assessment still accurate 6 weeks later as Matt's actual level shifts) has no gold-reference check available at all under this constraint. Mitigate by treating the probe's output as a **prior that updates from in-app task performance** (e.g., if "B1"-rated Matt is resolving every scenario task trivially and never triggering pushed-output prompts, that's itself signal the initial estimate was too low) rather than a one-shot ground truth that's never revisited.

### 7.4 The underlying feature/ML literature (context for the above)

For automated estimation from free speech generally, the literature converges on a feature set:

- **Fluency features**: speech rate (syllables or words per minute) is repeatedly reported as **the single most reliable indicator of proficiency** across studies; also filled-pause frequency, unfilled-pause (silence) frequency and duration, and mean length of run between pauses. **[well-established]** Source: [Multi-CEFR-level fluency/accuracy study 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/00238309251393170).
- **Lexical features**: type-token ratio (TTR) and number-of-distinct-words (NDW) for lexical diversity; lexical sophistication measured via CEFR-tagged vocabulary-profile coverage (e.g., English Vocabulary Profile-style resources — Spanish equivalents exist via Instituto Cervantes' *Plan Curricular* vocabulary lists). **[well-established as correlational features, contested as causally meaningful without normalization]**
- **Syntactic complexity**: mean length of utterance/sentence, dependency-tree depth, subordination frequency.
- **Accuracy/error rate**: normalized error counts per T-unit or per 100 words.
- **ML approaches**: supervised classifiers (Random Forest, Gradient-Boosted Trees, SVM, small neural nets) trained on these features achieve strong discrimination between adjacent CEFR levels (one cited study: GBT AUC up to 0.916 for A1→A2, 0.904 for A2→B1) — though note these numbers degrade for finer-grained or higher-level (B2→C1) distinctions where features converge and human raters themselves show more disagreement. Source: [CEFR level assessment overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/cefr-level-assessment), [Predicting CEFR levels via lexical and syntactic complexity](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354773690_Predicting_CEFR_Levels_in_L2_Oral_Speech_Based_on_Lexical_and_Syntactic_Complexity), [Lexical complexity across CEFR levels](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381306129_Lexical_Complexity_And_Language_Proficiency_An_Investigation_of_Indices_Across_Cefr_Levels).

### 7.5 Which metrics are gameable — this matters a lot since these may feed an RL reward signal

- **TTR is highly gameable and length-sensitive.** Raw TTR mechanically decreases as utterance length increases (more tokens → more repetition of function words), so a naive reward tied to raw TTR either rewards short utterances (inflating TTR artificially) or needs a length-corrected variant (MTLD, HD-D, vocd-D) — using raw TTR as an RL reward is a textbook reward-hacking vector: **the model/learner could learn to pad with rare words or truncate utterances rather than actually communicate better.** **[flag: gameable, use corrected lexical-diversity measures instead]**
- **Speech rate is gameable via mumbling/skipping disfluency markers or dropping function words** — a system optimizing pure WPM without an intelligibility/accuracy gate would reward fast garbage over slower correct speech. Must be paired with an ASR-confidence or comprehensibility check, not used standalone.
- **Pause frequency is gameable in the opposite direction** — a learner (or a model gaming its own reward) could learn to fill pauses with meaningless filler ("eh", "bueno", "o sea") to reduce measured "silence," which is exactly the surface behavior of a disfluent speaker mimicking fluent-speaker patterns without underlying gains. Needs to distinguish filled-pause-as-genuine-cognitive-load vs. filled-pause-as-reward-hack.
- **Lexical sophistication (rare/advanced-word usage) is gameable via word-dropping** — a learner could memorize and insert a few C1-tagged words into otherwise simple speech to inflate a sophistication score without any underlying gain in usable vocabulary; this is the direct language-app analog of "keyword stuffing."
- **Error rate is the least gameable of the common metrics** but is the hardest to compute reliably from ASR output on a small on-device model — errors require grammatical parsing, and ASR transcription errors get conflated with production errors.
- **Not gameable in the same way, and underused**: task-outcome success (did the information-gap actually get resolved correctly — §5 tasks with objective ground truth) is a much more robust reward signal than any linguistic surface metric, because it's grounded in whether communication *actually worked*, not in producing statistically "proficient-looking" output. **This should be the primary reward signal, with linguistic metrics as auxiliary/diagnostic signals, not the reward itself** — see Design Implications.

## 8. Design implications

Opinionated, specific, meant to be argued with. Trip is under 3 weeks away, so this is split into what must exist in that window (ruthless — if it's not on this list, it doesn't ship before the trip) and what's a longer-lived-version concern that would be premature optimization right now. Every item in both lists assumes the hard constraint: **Mac-only, fully offline, no cloud API calls, no phone app, no native-speaker-in-the-loop** — where that constraint forces a specific implementation choice, it's called out inline.

### (a) Must be in the 2-3 week v0 — be ruthless

1. **Ship the spoken CEFR placement probe (§7) as literally the first screen of the app.** This is now the highest-leverage single thing to build: without it, every other exercise is guessing at difficulty. Minimum viable version: C-test (exact-match scoring, trivial) + elicited imitation (ASR-diff scoring) only — **cut the picture-description and monologue tasks from v0 if time is tight**, since their scoring needs the riskier LLM-judge step (§7.3) and their contribution is weak-area *profiling*, not the core level estimate. A CEFR band + "your EI reproduction breaks down on X" from just the two objectively-scorable tasks is a legitimate, shippable v0, even if thinner than the full 4-task battery in §7.1.

2. **Build the on-device pipeline once, correctly, since everything else depends on it: local ASR (whisper.cpp / MLX Whisper, quantized small/base model) → text → scoring.** This is not a "nice architecture" concern, it's the load-bearing plumbing for the placement probe, elicited imitation, and any spoken exercise at all. Get latency and accuracy validated on the M3 *first*, before building exercise content on top of an unproven pipeline — an offline ASR pipeline that's too slow or too inaccurate silently breaks every downstream scoring decision.

3. **Pick 2, not 10, forcing-function mechanics from the §5 table — elicited imitation and one time-pressured scenario task.** Elicited imitation because it's dual-purpose (also the placement probe) and cheapest to auto-score (ASR-diff, no LLM judgment). One scenario task (e.g., #1 time-pressured roleplay or #13 comprehension-checked interaction) because it's the actual forcing-function thesis in its purest form and is only medium-difficulty to auto-eval (task-completion check, not subjective quality). Do **not** build the 4/3/2 technique, retelling, debate, or register-drills for v0 — all are good ideas (kept in (b)) but add scoring complexity or content-authoring time the 3-week window doesn't have.

4. **Score only what's objectively scorable in v0; defer every LLM-judge-graded task.** Per §7.3/§7.5, a small on-device model grading open-ended speech quality is the single riskiest, most sycophancy-prone piece of this whole design, and there's no gold-reference/human-in-the-loop available to catch it drifting lenient. v0 should only include tasks with a fixed answer key (C-test), a diffable target (elicited imitation), or a checkable state (did the scenario resolve) — this is a direct, load-bearing consequence of the offline/no-human-grader constraint, not just a nice-to-have simplification.

5. **Default all content, TTS voice, and ASR vocabulary bias to Peninsular/Castilian Spanish now.** Zero marginal cost to decide this at day one vs. retrofitting later (§6); getting it wrong means every recorded exercise and scenario script has to be redone.

6. **Track weak-areas as a flat per-construction list, not a decay model.** A real forgetting-curve/attrition model (§1, §8b-1) needs weeks of repeated-exposure data this build doesn't have yet. v0 just needs: after the placement probe and each exercise, append/update a simple list ("subjunctive: weak, vosotros comprehension: untested, transactional vocab: strong") that the next session's task selection reads from. This is a lookup table, not a model — resist the urge to build FSRS-style scheduling before there's enough usage data to schedule against.

7. **Do not build any gamification layer (streaks, XP, leaderboards) for v0 — there's no audience for it (single user, 3 weeks) and the evidence doesn't support it for speaking anyway.** This one is free — it's the absence of work, not additional work — but worth stating explicitly since it's the most tempting thing to copy from existing apps out of habit. If any completion signal is shown at all, tie it to "scenario resolved" (§5), never to session count or streak.

8. **Log every exercise attempt with enough raw detail to analyze later, even though analysis itself waits for (b).** Cheap to do now (just don't throw away audio/transcripts/scores), expensive to reconstruct retroactively. This is the only (b)-adjacent item worth doing in the v0 window, because it's pure data-hygiene with near-zero build cost today and it's what makes the "instrument the open questions" item in (b) possible at all.

### (b) Longer-lived version only — explicitly deferred, don't build yet

1. **Real RL loop with task-outcome-success as primary reward, linguistic metrics as auxiliary/diagnostic signal only (§7.5).** This needs enough logged sessions (see (a)-8) to have a reward surface worth optimizing against at all; building an RL loop against a handful of pre-trip sessions is premature and risks overfitting to noise.

2. **Per-skill/per-construction decay modeling and FSRS-style spaced scheduling for constructions** (§1, §3). Genuinely needs a multi-week usage history to fit against, and is explicitly a *post-trip maintenance-mode* concern (§1's "trip is a spike, not a cure") more than a pre-trip acquisition concern — the 3-week window is dominated by forcing new production, not spacing reviews of things not yet learned.

3. **The full stakes-escalation arc across a training program** (§4, §8a's "one scenario task" is the seed of this) — deliberately ramping time pressure, unpredictability, and simulated social cost over many sessions is a real design commitment worth doing right, not squeezing into week one. v0's single scenario task should already run under real time pressure, but a *graduated* arc requires more content and more sessions than 3 weeks allows.

4. **Full register/pragmatics scenario library** (tuteo norms, bar-ordering scripts, formal-vs-informal register switching, §5 #11, §6). High product-differentiation value but content-authoring-heavy; v0's single scenario task can be *one* Spain-specific script (e.g., ordering at a bar) as a taste of this, but the systematic library is a post-trip or ongoing-content investment.

5. **The full 4-task CEFR placement battery (C-test + EI + picture description + monologue) and richer weak-area profiling**, once the LLM-judge leniency problem (§7.3) has an actual answer — e.g., a small curated set of CEFR-tagged Spanish exemplars baked in as calibration anchors, tested against Matt's own known cases over time. v0 ships the 2-task cut (§8a-1); the full battery is worth building once there's time to get the anti-leniency design right rather than shipping it undertested days before departure.

6. **Explicit post-trip maintenance mode** (§1, §8a's decay-list is its precursor). By definition this only matters *after* the trip, and its design should be informed by what actually happened during the trip and the acquisition phase — building it now would be designing blind.

7. **Instrumenting and actually analyzing the two open research questions flagged throughout this doc** — whether low-anxiety AI practice transfers to high-stakes human interaction (§4), and whether spaced scheduling helps productive skill at all vs. just vocabulary (§3). The logging in (a)-8 makes this possible later; the analysis itself is not a pre-trip task.
