# 02b — Model Survey (appendix to 02-local-slm.md §1)

Scope: open-weight LLMs roughly 1B–8B params, evaluated as an offline
Peninsular-Spanish conversation partner on a MacBook Air M3 — 16GB unified
memory, 10 GPU cores, fanless, ~11GB usable for the whole stack (LLM + ASR +
TTS). Compiled 2026-09-12 via live fetches (HuggingFace model cards/org pages,
Ollama library, mlx-community search). WebSearch was unavailable this session
(budget exhausted); everything below comes from WebFetch of specific pages, so
coverage is narrower than a full search would give — treat absence of a model
here as "not checked," not "doesn't exist."

Every cell not backed by a fetched source in this session is marked
**UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. Verdict

**Realistic candidates at ~11GB usable, all-in with ASR+TTS running alongside:**

1. **Gemma 3 (1B or 4B, `-it-qat` tag)** — QAT builds are explicitly built to preserve BF16-like quality at ~3x smaller footprint; the 1B fits trivially, the 4B is still workable inside 11GB with a quantized ASR/TTS stack. Both are Ollama-native and mlx-community-native. Strongest overall combination of size, license permissiveness relative to gate-free download, and confirmed 2026 availability.
2. **Llama 3.2 3B** — already the prior local baseline per the task's own benchmark note; small, MLX/Ollama-native, officially lists Spanish as a supported language with a published MMLU number (55.1%). License is custom/gated (Llama 3.2 Community License) but not restrictive at this usage scale.
3. **Qwen3 4B or 8B** — Apache 2.0 (cleanest license of the bunch), native Ollama and MLX support, huge language coverage claim, but **no multilingual benchmark number could be confirmed** this session — treat its Spanish quality as unverified until tested directly.
4. **BSC Salamandra 2B (instruct)** — the only Spanish-native-training candidate that plausibly fits the memory budget. 7B is marginal (only a bf16 mlx build was found, no quantized mlx-community version — would need a self-made GGUF/MLX quant or an Ollama community quant to fit 11GB). The 2B size has a dedicated Ollama tag (`hdnh2006/salamandra-2b-instruct`) and inherits the family's explicit training emphasis on Spain's co-official languages, which is the single most direct match to "Peninsular Spanish" of anything surveyed — but note its published benchmarks are commonsense/paraphrase tasks, not conversational-register or dialect-fidelity tasks, so this is a hypothesis, not a confirmed win.

**Worth testing specifically for Peninsular fidelity** (vosotros, distinción, "vale," ordenador/coche/móvil):

Given the task's own prior finding that Peninsular fidelity on this machine ranked **inverse to model size** (gemma2:2b > llama3.2:3b > qwen2.5:1.5b), the small-size-first hypothesis should extend to:

- **Gemma 3 1B and 4B** (the direct successors to the gemma2:2b that won the prior round) — highest-priority retest.
- **Llama 3.2 3B** — direct successor to the previously-tested llama3.2:3b, confirm the ranking holds on the newer weights.
- **Salamandra 2B-instruct** — the one model in this survey actually trained with deliberate weighting toward Spain's co-official languages rather than generic "Spanish" web text; if the small-model-wins pattern holds *and* Iberian-specific training helps, this is the theoretical best case for vosotros/distinción/vale over voseo or Latin American lexicon. Highest-value single test to run given the inverse-size finding.
- **Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B** as a low end control, mirroring why qwen2.5:1.5b was the weakest performer last time — worth confirming whether Qwen3's much larger claimed language coverage (100+ languages vs. Qwen2.5) changes that result, or whether the pattern (broad multilingual pretraining diluting a specific dialect) repeats.

**Explicitly not worth pursuing at this budget:** Ministral (non-commercial license by default is a real constraint, and it is not specifically Iberian-trained), Mistral Small (24B, out of budget), Phi-4-mini (MIT license and Spanish support are fine, but nothing in its card suggests dialect-level Iberian tuning — treat as a fallback, not a priority), ALIA (same training family as Salamandra but no confirmed Ollama/MLX packaging found — revisit only if Salamandra underperforms and someone is willing to quantize ALIA-7B by hand), Latxa (wrong language — Basque, not Spanish), Gemma 4 and Qwen3.8 (too new to have community tooling or verified benchmarks yet; flag for a follow-up pass in a few months once Ollama/mlx-community catch up).

**Biggest open gap:** no source in this session produced a benchmark that isolates *register/dialect* (Peninsular vs. Latin American Spanish) rather than raw Spanish-language competence — every published number above (MMLU-es, XStoryCloze-es, Spanish commonsense/QA) measures whether the model can use Spanish at all, not whether it defaults to vosotros/vale/ordenador over ustedes/dale/computadora. That gap is exactly why the task's own local benchmark exists and should stay the primary source of truth over any of the numbers in this table.
