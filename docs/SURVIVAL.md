# SURVIVAL SHEET — Spain

Peninsular Spanish. Screenshot the top two sections. `th` = English "th" in "think" (Spain's `c`/`z`).

---

## 1. EMERGENCY — dial 112 for everything

`112` works for ambulance, police, and fire in Spain — one number, no need to pick.

- **Ambulance** — Una ambulancia, por favor. [OO-na am-boo-LAN-thya por fa-BOR]
- **Police** — La policía. [la po-lee-THEE-a]
- **Fire** — Los bomberos. [los bom-BEH-ros]
- **I need help** — Necesito ayuda. [neh-theh-SEE-to ah-YOO-da]
- **I'm lost** — Me he perdido. [meh eh per-DEE-do]
- **I've been robbed** — Me han robado. [meh an ro-BA-do]
- **Call a doctor** — Llame a un médico. [YA-meh a oon MEH-dee-ko]
- **I'm allergic to ___** — Soy alérgico/a a ___. [soy ah-LER-hee-ko/ka a ___]
- **I'm diabetic** — Soy diabético/a. [soy dya-BEH-tee-ko/ka]
- **He/she isn't breathing** — No respira. [no res-PEE-ra]

## 2. MEDICAL / PHARMACY

**⚠ `constipado` = I have a cold. It does NOT mean constipated. This is the highest-consequence false friend on this sheet — saying it to a pharmacist gets you cold medicine, which is what you want, but never use it to mean the other thing.**

- **I have a cold** — Estoy constipado/a. [es-TOY kon-stee-PA-do/da]
- **I'm constipated (the actual meaning)** — Estoy estreñido/a. [es-TOY es-treh-NYEE-do/da]

**Symptoms:**
- Headache — Me duele la cabeza. [meh DWEH-leh la ka-BEH-tha]
- Stomach ache — Me duele el estómago. [meh DWEH-leh el es-TOH-ma-go]
- Sore throat — Me duele la garganta. [meh DWEH-leh la gar-GAN-ta]
- Fever — Tengo fiebre. [TEN-go FYEH-breh]
- I'm dizzy — Estoy mareado/a. [es-TOY ma-reh-A-do/da]
- I don't feel well — No me encuentro bien. [no meh en-KWEN-tro byen]

**Body parts:**
- Head — la cabeza [la ka-BEH-tha]
- Stomach — el estómago [el es-TOH-ma-go]
- Throat — la garganta [la gar-GAN-ta]
- Arm — el brazo [el BRA-tho]
- Leg — la pierna [la PYER-na]
- Chest — el pecho [el PEH-cho]
- Back — la espalda [la es-PAL-da]
- Ankle — el tobillo [el to-BEE-yo]

- **Do I need a prescription?** — ¿Necesito receta? [neh-theh-SEE-to reh-THEH-ta]
- **Is there a pharmacy near here?** — ¿Hay una farmacia por aquí cerca? [ai OO-na far-MA-thya por a-KEE THER-ka]
- **I'm allergic to penicillin** — Soy alérgico/a a la penicilina. [soy ah-LER-hee-ko/ka a la peh-nee-thee-LEE-na]

## 3. THE TEN SENTENCES THAT FIX EVERYTHING

1. **Speak more slowly, please** — ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? [PWEH-deh ah-BLAR mas des-PA-thyo por fa-BOR]
2. **Repeat that** — ¿Puede repetirlo? [PWEH-deh reh-peh-TEER-lo]
3. **I didn't understand** — Perdone, no le he entendido. [per-DOH-neh no leh eh en-ten-DEE-do]
4. **How do you say this?** — ¿Cómo se dice esto en español? [KOH-mo seh DEE-theh ES-to en es-pa-NYOL]
5. **What does that mean?** — ¿Qué significa eso? [keh seeg-nee-FEE-ka EH-so]
6. **I'm learning Spanish** — Estoy aprendiendo español. [es-TOY ah-pren-DYEN-do es-pa-NYOL]
7. **Do you speak English? (last resort)** — ¿Habla inglés? [AH-bla een-GLES]
8. **Can you write it down?** — ¿Me lo puede escribir? [meh lo PWEH-deh es-kree-BEER]
9. **I don't know** — No sé. [no seh]
10. **One moment, please** — Un momento, por favor. [oon mo-MEN-to por fa-BOR]

- **Return ticket** — Un billete de ida y vuelta. [oon bee-YEH-teh deh EE-da ee BWEL-ta]

## 8. THINGS YOU WILL HEAR, NOT SAY

Staff fire these fast — recognize them, don't try to say them back:

- **¿Qué te pongo?** — "What can I get you?" [keh teh PON-go] (bar greeting)
- **¿Algo más?** — "Anything else?" [AL-go mas]
- **¿Para tomar aquí o para llevar?** — "For here or to go?" [PA-ra to-MAR a-KEE o PA-ra yeh-BAR]
- **¿Con tarjeta o en efectivo?** — "Card or cash?" [kon tar-HEH-ta o en eh-fek-TEE-bo]
- **¿Tiene cambio?** — "Do you have change?" [TYEH-neh KAM-byo]
- **Siguiente** — "Next" (queue) [see-GYEN-teh]
- **Dígame** — "Yes? / How can I help?" (phone or counter) [DEE-ga-meh]

## 9. CULTURE NOTES THAT PREVENT MISTAKES

- Lunch is 14:00–16:00, dinner is 21:00 or later — restaurants are often shut in between.
- Small shops close midday, roughly 14:00–17:00.
- Tipping is minimal — rounding up is normal, nobody expects 15–20%.
- `Hasta luego` [AS-ta LWEH-go] is said to everyone, including strangers you'll never see again — cashiers, drivers, anyone.
- Entering a small shop: greet the room — `Buenos días` [BWEH-nos DEE-as] or `Buenas` [BWEH-nas] — before asking for anything.
- Ask for `los servicios` [los ser-BEE-thyos] or `los aseos`, not `el baño`, in bars, stations, and other public places.
